What is the cloud, actually?

§ 01 The short answer

The cloud, in one paragraph

Quick answer

The cloud is computing you rent instead of own: always-on computers in data centers, with fast connections and public internet addresses, run by companies like Amazon, Google, Microsoft and DigitalOcean. You pay for what you use — often by the second.

“Deploying to the cloud” means copying your app from your laptop (your localhost) onto one of those machines, so anyone can reach it, anytime, even with your laptop closed.

§ 05 The speed of light is a budget

Why distance matters

Light in fiber travels about 200 km per millisecond — two-thirds of its speed in a vacuum. That sounds instant until you do the math: a round trip from New York to London covers about 11,000 km, so it can never take less than ~56 ms, no matter how fast the servers are.

That's why clouds have regions on every continent, and why you put your app near your users. And it's why localhost feels impossibly fast: its round trip has no distance in it at all.

§ 07 The landscape

Kinds of clouds

All of them rent you computers in data centers. They differ in how much they hide from you.

Hyperscalers

AWS · Google Cloud · Azure

Hundreds of services in dozens of regions. Maximum power and flexibility — and a learning curve to match.

Developer clouds

DigitalOcean · Hetzner · Akamai

Simple virtual servers and managed databases with predictable pricing. Great when you want a real server without the sprawl.

App platforms

Vercel · Netlify · Render · Railway · Fly.io

Push your code; they handle servers, scaling and HTTPS. The default for many web apps.

Backend as a service

Supabase · Firebase

A managed database, logins and file storage you plug into, instead of running your own backend.

Agent-first

Cloudish.ai

No dashboard at all: an API built for AI coding agents to deploy containers end-to-end, from one sentence. This site runs on it.

§ 08 FAQ

Cloud questions, answered

What is the cloud, in simple terms?

Computers in data centers that you rent over the internet instead of buying. They stay on all the time, have fast connections and public addresses, and you pay only for what you use.

Where is the cloud, physically?

In data centers: warehouse-sized buildings full of servers, grouped into regions near big cities on every inhabited continent. When you deploy, you usually choose a region close to your users.

Is the cloud just someone else's computer?

Yes — millions of them, with redundant power, cooling, networking and security teams you'd never build yourself, available in seconds and billed by usage. “Someone else's computer” is exactly the point.

What's the difference between IaaS, PaaS and SaaS?

They differ in how much you manage. With IaaS you rent virtual machines and manage the operating system and everything above it. With PaaS you hand over your code and the platform runs it. With SaaS you just use finished software, like Gmail.

Why does my app need the cloud if it works on localhost?

Localhost only works on your own computer and only while it's on. A cloud server has a public address and never sleeps, so anyone can use your app from anywhere, at any time.

Is the cloud expensive?

It doesn't have to be. Many platforms have free tiers, bill by the second, and scale idle apps to zero, so a small personal project can cost cents a day or nothing at all. Costs grow with traffic and resources.
